# Optimize usage

Usage Trends tracks your account's billed snapshots over time. Go to the billing page and click **Trends (beta)** to see snapshots, builds, and TurboSnap efficiency over time.

Use the dashboard to diagnose usage spikes then work through the questions below, in order, to optimize your usage.

![Example of a Usage Trends dashboard showing the Billed snapshots chart](../../images/usage-trends.png)

## When did the usage spike start, and in which project?

Start with the **Billed Snapshots** and **Billed Snapshots per Project** charts. You're looking for the week usage changed, and ideally the one project it changed in.

![Billed Snapshots chart shows how many billed snapshots each project generated in the previous 156 weeks](../../images/usage-trends-billed-snapshots.png)

Look for whether most of your usage comes from one project, or whether one project shows the same pattern of change as your account overall. Filter the dashboard to that project to make other charts easier to read and to better understand what's driving the change.

You can filter the dashboard by clicking the project name in the **Billed Snapshots per Project** donut chart, or by using the **App ID** dropdown in the top left of the dashboard.

![Example of filtering the Usage Trends dashboard by clicking on a project](../../images/usage-trends-filter.png)

## Did build count go up?

The main usage drivers are build count and snapshots per build. To diagnose your usage, check whether the Builds line and the bars track each other on the **Billed Snapshots** chart.

- **Lines up closely:** your usage increase is almost certainly driven by more builds. Move on to ["Are builds outpacing PRs?"](#are-builds-outpacing-prs) to understand why build volume grew.

- **Doesn't line up:** billed snapshots per build is likely the bigger contributor. Skip ahead to ["Are snapshots per build climbing?"](#are-snapshots-per-build-climbing).

## Are builds outpacing PRs?

More PRs, especially PRs with changes, means more builds and greater Chromatic usage. Use the **Build count** chart to compare builds against PRs.

If builds don't closely line up with PRs, it's likely that a structural change is causing additional builds. For example, your team commits many times per PR, or builds run on a long-lived branch like `main`.

![Build count chart breaks the build volume down to builds on feature or default branches. A “ default” branch is a branch with a name commonly considered for a trunk branch: main, master, trunk, dev , devel, development, staging, prod, production.](../../images/usage-trends-build-count.png)

## Are snapshots per build climbing?

**Total Snapshots per Build** naturally rise as you add more tests. A gradual increase usually reflects growing coverage. A sharp jump typically points to a specific change, such as enabling a new test type (accessibility/visual) or adding more browsers across the board. If that isn't the case, check for a misconfiguration in your CI setup.

![Billed Snapshots per Build chart breaks down billed snapshot usage historically into the contribution of various types of snapshots. The line charts the total number of builds in the time period.](../../images/usage-trends-billed-snapshots-per-build.png)

[TurboSnap](/docs/turbosnap) is meant to offset the gradual increase. If TurboSnap is working well, your **Snapshot billing rate** should come down as **Total snapshots per Build** go up. That's because TurboSnap copies or bypasses unchanged snapshots instead of capturing them again.

**Snapshot billing rate** is the ratio of billed snapshots to total snapshots. Captured snapshots cost a full billed snapshot and copied or bypassed ones cost 0.2 billed snapshots. So the more TurboSnap replaces captures with copies or bypasses, the lower your rate.

A rate of 1.0 means every snapshot was captured again. In other words, TurboSnap isn't working as expected. If TurboSnap is working well, this number should be lower.

## Is TurboSnap bailing too often?

[Bailed builds](/docs/turbosnap/troubleshooting#what-do-the-turbosnap-bail-reasons-in-my-usage-report-mean) drive up **Billed Snapshots per Build** even when **Total Snapshots per Build** are stable.

TurboSnap effectiveness is the amount of copied or bypassed snapshots relative to captured ones. Your goal is to reduce the "bail" segments of the chart below and grow the "copied" and "bypassed" segments.

![TurboSnap effectiveness shows how many snapshots are being copied or bypassed rather than captured](../../images/usage-trends-ts-effectiveness.png)

### Why TurboSnap bails affect your bill

TurboSnap copies a snapshot only when it can confidently determine that nothing affecting that test changed, based on your Git history and dependency graph.

When it can't establish that (because of how your CI checks out history, barrel files, how your Storybook is configured, or other reasons), it bails. In that case, Chromatic captures every test instead of copying the unchanged ones, and the build is billed at the full captured rate.

### Identifying the root cause of bails

Bail reasons are listed per build in the [usage report CSV export](/docs/billing/usage-reports#export-usage-data-as-a-csv). Use it to dig into specific builds rather than trends over time.

If bails are a significant share of your builds, work through [TurboSnap troubleshooting](/docs/turbosnap/troubleshooting) to resolve the specific issues.
