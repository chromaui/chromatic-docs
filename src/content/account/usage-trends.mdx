# Understand usage trends

The Usage trends dashboard charts your account's [billed snapshot](/docs/billing) usage over time so you can diagnose a spike, understand what changed, and decide what to do next. This guide walks through that diagnosis step by step using the data available within the dashboard.

## Diagnose a usage spike

### Find when and where usage changed

Start with the **Billed Snapshots** and **Billed Snapshots per Project** charts to find the period, and ideally the single project, where usage changed significantly.

Look for whether most of your usage comes from one project, or whether one project shows the same pattern of change as your account overall. Filter the dashboard to that project to make other charts easier to read and to better understand what's driving the change.

![Billed Snapshots chart shows how many billed snapshots each project generated in the previous 156 weeks](../../images/usage-trends-billed-snapshots.png)

### Check whether usage change correlates with build volume

Look at whether the builds line matches the bars on the **Billed Snapshots** chart:

- **Lines up closely:** your usage increase is almost certainly driven by more builds. Move on to [build volume vs. velocity](#compare-build-volume-with-development-velocity) to understand why build volume grew.
- **Doesn't line up:** billed snapshots per build is likely the bigger contributor. Skip ahead to [billed snapshots per build](#look-closely-at-billed-snapshots-per-build).

### Compare build volume with development velocity

Use the **Build count** chart to compare build volume increases against velocity increases. If your team's velocity is increasing (i.e., more PRs, especially PRs with changes), it's natural to see more builds and greater Chromatic usage.

On the other hand, if build counts don't closely line up with PR counts, there may be a structural change (e.g., more commits per PR or more builds on main) that could be addressed.

![Build count chart breaks the build volume down to builds on feature or default branches. We chart the number of PRs, considering every non-main branch with a build on it as a PR (this may overcount slightly compared to your Git provider). A “ default” branch is a branch with a name commonly considered for a trunk branch: main, master, trunk, dev , devel, development, staging, prod, production.](../../images/usage-trends-build-count.png)

### Look closely at billed snapshots per build

**Total snapshots per build** naturally rise as you add more tests. A gradual increase usually reflects growing coverage. A sharp jump typically points to a specific change, such as enabling a new test type (accessibility/visual) or adding more browsers across the board. If that was not the case, then check for a misconfiguration in your CI setup.

[TurboSnap](/docs/turbosnap) is intended to offset that increase. Usually, as your total snapshots per build go up, your **Snapshot billing rate** should decrease. If it's not, then investigate TurboSnap's effectiveness next.

![Billed Snapshots per Build chart breaks down billed snapshot usage historically into the contribution of various types of snapshots. The line charts the total number of builds in the time period.](../../images/usage-trends-billed-snapshots-per-build.png)

### Check TurboSnap effectiveness for your project

TurboSnap's effectiveness has the biggest impact on the snapshot billing rate. A high number of bailed builds drives up billed snapshots per build, even if your total snapshots per build is stable.

TurboSnap's effectiveness is measured by the amount of copied or bypassed snapshots relative to captured snapshots. The more snapshots TurboSnap can copy or bypass instead of recapturing, the lower your billing rate.

If bails are a significant share of your builds, then work through [TurboSnap troubleshooting](/docs/turbosnap/troubleshooting) to resolve the specific issues.

![TurboSnap effectiveness shows how many snapshots are being copied or bypassed rather than captured](../../images/usage-trends-ts-effectiveness.png)

## Why TurboSnap bails affect your bill

TurboSnap only copies a snapshot when it can confidently determine that nothing affecting that test changed, based on your Git history and dependency graph.

When it can't establish that (because of how your CI checks out history, barrel files, how your Storybook is configured, or other reasons), it [bails](/docs/turbosnap/troubleshooting#what-do-the-turbosnap-bail-reasons-in-my-usage-report-mean). In that case, Chromatic falls back to capturing every test instead of copying the unchanged ones. That increases the snapshot billing rate because a build that would otherwise be mostly copied snapshots is billed at the full captured rate instead.

You can also see bail reasons per build in your [usage report CSV export](/docs/billing/usage-reports#export-usage-data-as-a-csv) if you want to dig into specific builds rather than trends over time.
